# Monetary Policy and State Government Economic Performance

## Research Publication

**Title:** Effect of Monetary Policy on Economic Performance of State Governments in South-East, Nigeria 2015–2023

**Authors:** Nchedochukwu Chinwendu Ezenwegbu, Ngozi Ewuim, Rosemary Anazodo and Ogechukwu Nwankwo.

**Journal:** Journal of the Management Sciences, Volume 61, Issue 9, December 2024, pages 381–403.

## Overview

This study examines relationships between monetary policy indicators and state government revenue generation and infrastructure development in South-East Nigeria.

It covers Abia, Anambra, Ebonyi, Enugu and Imo states, with a study focus on 2015–2023 and questionnaire evidence collected in 2024.

## Research Objectives

1. Examine the relationship between interest rate fluctuations and revenue generation.
2. Assess the relationship between exchange rate fluctuations and infrastructure development.
3. Examine the relationship between inflation and infrastructure development.
4. Assess the relationship between broad money supply and revenue generation.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Survey research.
- **Sample:** 400 questionnaires distributed; 397 completed and returned.
- **Instrument:** Structured questionnaire using a five-point Likert scale.
- **Validation:** Face and content validation.
- **Reliability:** Cronbach’s alpha of 0.78.
- **Analysis:** Pearson correlation at a 5% significance level, as specified in the methodology section.

## Findings Reported in the Article

| Relationship examined | Reported correlation | Reported significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interest rate fluctuations and revenue generation | 1.000 | p < 0.001 |
| Exchange rate fluctuations and infrastructure development | 0.899 | p < 0.001 |
| Inflation and infrastructure development | 0.832 | p < 0.001 |
| Broad money supply and revenue generation | 0.782 | p < 0.001 |

Respondents associated macroeconomic fluctuations with borrowing costs, investment conditions, infrastructure costs and fiscal planning.

## Policy Recommendations

- Incorporate interest rate, exchange rate and inflation risks into state budgets and infrastructure planning.
- Strengthen coordination between fiscal and monetary authorities.
- Build state government capacity to interpret macroeconomic trends.
- Engage businesses, investors and financial institutions in managing economic risks.

## Interpretation and Limitations

These results summarise the published article and have not been independently reproduced.

The questionnaire-based correlations describe associations and respondent perceptions; they do not establish causal effects. A positive correlation does not necessarily mean that higher inflation or greater exchange rate volatility improves infrastructure.

The perfect interest-rate correlation of 1.000 requires verification against the original data and analytical outputs.

The abstract mentions regression analysis, while the methodology specifies Pearson correlation. This summary follows the methodology section.

## Repository Scope

This repository currently provides a research summary. The underlying dataset, analysis scripts and statistical output files are not included.
